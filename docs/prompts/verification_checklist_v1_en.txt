General decision-verification checklist:
1. Identify the user's objective, mandatory conditions, and preferences used to compare eligible options. Distinguish facts, conditions, permissions, and suggestions; do not introduce assumptions absent from the input.
2. Check candidates against all applicable conditions. Local satisfaction is not overall satisfaction; retain circumstances that could affect the purchase decision.
3. Verify amounts, units, inclusive and strict thresholds, and the order of charges and compensation. Valid simplification, grouping, or bounds may reduce repeated calculation, but unverified approximations cannot replace exact boundary judgments.
4. Compare eligible options and verify that the selection and reported utility agree. Refuse only when no eligible positive-value option exists; failing to find one is not itself evidence of infeasibility.
5. Before submitting, check all relevant requirements, potentially better eligible options, and the evidence for a refusal. Return only the requested answer format and a brief reason.
These are general natural-language checks. Do not generate programs, functions, expression specifications, or pseudocode, use tools, or ask the user for further confirmation.
