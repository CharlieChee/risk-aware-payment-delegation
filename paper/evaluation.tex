\subsection{Tasks, Comparisons, and Scoring}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Contractual boundaries.}
We construct 200 tasks from 20 templates in four families: coupon cancellation, insured cancellation, threshold-dependent returns, and service replacement. Contracts combine overlapping groups, compensation layers, shipping charges, and item/order caps. Catalogues contain 128--1,024 candidates; Appendix~\ref{app:data} specifies attributes, events, and monetary units.

Each template contributes five feasible/infeasible pairs. A feasible task has exactly one authorized positive-value candidate. Raising its fee puts its worst loss one monetary subunit above the limit, with other attributes and offer order fixed. The correct decision changes from purchase to refusal. These pairs isolate responsiveness to authorization boundaries \citep{checklist,contrastsets}. Inputs and references were frozen before primary calls; separate cash-ledger and expression calculations, random-attribute checks, and hand-calculated witnesses verify the contract semantics (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\paragraph{Decision workflows.}
\direct\ receives the request, schema, catalogue, and the joint-feasibility, worst-value, and refusal rules of Section~\ref{sec:method}. It returns a decision and utility without external execution. \ours\ separates preparation of the state-dependent conditions from fixed catalogue evaluation. The preparation stage receives the request and schema but excludes the catalogue. Conditions use exact arithmetic and retain clause scope, compensation order, and value binding. Evaluation semantics and observed compilations are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The shared decision policy fixes the target behavior for both methods; the comparison measures how reliably each complete workflow realizes that behavior. Appendix~\ref{app:execution} reports the textual-checklist baseline and sensitivity analyses.

\paragraph{Outcomes.}
An \emph{exact decision} agrees with the reference and, for acceptance, returns authorized maximizers with correct utility. \emph{Safe completion} is exact acceptance on a feasible task. \emph{Unsafe acceptance} selects a reference-infeasible offer; \emph{false refusal} rejects an available authorized positive-value purchase. \emph{Other} includes value errors and failed or unresolved execution, never credited as correct refusal. All outcomes remain in cohort denominators. Workflow comparisons are paired within configurations, with one semantic attempt and bounded transport retries (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Inputs and evaluation records accompany the artifact (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).

\subsection{Useful Completion at Authorization Boundaries}
\label{sec:boundaries}
All eleven primary configurations improve exactness and safe completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals (Figure~\ref{fig:completion}). For DeepSeek low/high, exact decisions rise from 248/400 (62.0\%) to 369/400 (92.25\%). The gain is 30.25 percentage points, with a 95\% template-cluster bootstrap interval of $[22.75,37.25]$, retaining both settings within each template. Exact purchases rise from 100 to 186 across 200 feasible outcomes; unsafe acceptances fall from 79 to 9 and false refusals from 53 to 1. On these tasks, stronger safety accompanies more useful purchases and fewer missed opportunities.

Gains cover all four contract families and 18 of 20 templates at each DeepSeek setting. They vary across models: GLM high improves from 64/200 to 188/200, Sol medium from 137/200 to 196/200, and Opus medium from 142/200 to 194/200. Qwen Max low rises from 59 to 87 exact outcomes but retains 46 unsafe acceptances. Appendix~\ref{app:statistics} gives complete partitions and template results. These pairs test authorization boundaries; their unique feasible offers limit conclusions about ranking.

\subsection{Purchasing under Signaled Intent Ambiguity}
\label{sec:intent-decisions}
Figure~\ref{fig:intent} reports 294 outcomes from 147 matched task--configuration pairs covering ticket-holder reference, conditional hotel cancellation, and individual versus batch exchange-cost limits. Both methods receive the catalogue, ambiguity cues, and decision policy; SET's primary compilation instead withholds catalogues. Table~\ref{tab:intent-figure3} gives denominators.

SET improves exactness in all four configurations as risk limits, penalties, and the positive-value boundary vary. Qwen records six unsafe acceptances with SET and five with Direct, so better exactness does not ensure fewer unsafe actions in every panel. Separate checks illustrate common purchases, empty intersections, worst-case ranking, and the zero-value boundary (Appendix~\ref{app:intent-boundaries}); they do not establish a comparative accuracy advantage.

\paragraph{Checks on the workflow comparison.}
\label{sec:robustness}
On the 60-task reasoning panel, SET improves exactness in 17 of 18 Sol/Terra/Luna settings; Luna off falls from 25/60 to 13/60. Across six DeepSeek setting--language cells, Direct achieves 33--36/60 exact decisions and SET 55--59/60. Appendix~\ref{app:execution} gives these panels (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness}), textual-guidance and output-budget controls, and their scope. These checks assess the observed workflow gains, but do not establish that every permission encoded by a successful specification is correct. We next freeze the specification to examine that stronger requirement.

\subsection{Authorization beyond the Selected Purchase}
\label{sec:permissions}
\label{sec:components}
\paragraph{Frozen-specification test.}
We freeze 800 new requests from 20 templates in four contract families, each with 128 offers. DeepSeek Flash, GLM, and Qwen Max, all at low effort, receive requests, contracts, and schemas without catalogues. Catalogues are fixed before generation and stratified by whether their highest-reference-value offer is authorized (Appendix~\ref{app:availability}).

The primary stress test removes prefixes of the original generated ranking, without using reference labels to choose removals. Exposure requires an initially safe purchase to become unauthorized within eight removals, while an alternative remains permitted and positively valued under both generated conditions and references. Permissions, utilities, surviving attributes, and the selector stay fixed; replays make no model calls.

\paragraph{Hidden permissions and behavioral exposure.}
Across the planned cohort, \AvailHiddenPct\% produce safe purchases that conceal false admissions; the primary budget exposes \AvailExposedPct\%. Figure~\ref{fig:ablations} shows cumulative exposure by removal budget and its structural coverage. The exposed specifications span \AvailExposedRequests{} distinct requests, \AvailExposedTemplates{} templates, all four families, and all three configurations. Their minimum witnesses retain \AvailBackupMin{}--\AvailBackupMax{} viable authorized alternatives. Every exposed maximizer is unauthorized, so these transitions do not depend on mixed-authorization ties. Native-expression replay and independent contract calculations verify the witnesses.

\paragraph{Exposure from exact decisions.}
\AvailExactExposed{} exposed specifications initially select a reference maximizer and report its value correctly, spanning four templates and two configurations. In one GLM coupon-cancellation case, removing four high-ranked offers makes the frozen specification select an unauthorized purchase while 59 viable authorized alternatives remain. The specification omits a contractual charge when evaluating the newly selected offer, underestimating its loss and admitting it despite a reference loss above the authorized cap. Its original answer was correct; its permission for the alternative was not. Appendix~\ref{app:availability-witness} traces the omitted charge through the original expression and event ledger.

\paragraph{From intent to executable decisions.}
Candidate removal changes which admitted offer wins, not whether a surviving offer is authorized. The remaining viable alternatives are also permitted and positively valued by the specification itself. Thus the observed failure requires neither a new model response nor the loss of all useful authorized choices. Workflow gains and these failures address different requirements of the same delegation: explicit execution improves current decisions, but cannot by itself establish fidelity to the request. Examining the admitted domain reveals false permissions that a successful current purchase may never exercise. Appendix~\ref{app:availability-results} reports all templates, random removals, reverse transitions, and non-triggering cases.
