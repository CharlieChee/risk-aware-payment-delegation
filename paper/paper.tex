\begin{abstract}
Delegated purchasing requires an agent to pursue useful purchases within bounded authority. Uncertain interpretations and contractual outcomes complicate this task: choosing a convenient reading may exceed permission, while refusing every uncertain request discards useful options. We introduce SET, an executable workflow that requires authorization across retained states before ranking purchases by worst-case utility, accepting only a positive-value option without another user turn. On synthetic contractual-boundary tasks, SET improves exactness across all eleven primary model configurations by 8--64 percentage points, while reducing unauthorized acceptances and false refusals relative to direct decisions under the same policy. Yet better current decisions do not establish that generated permissions are correct. We analyze how ranking conceals false admissions and test this distinction by freezing generated conditions while changing candidate availability. Across 800 requests and three model configurations, removing at most eight high-ranked offers exposes unauthorized purchases on 48 requests spanning 15 templates and four contract families. Every witness retains alternatives permitted and positively valued under both generated conditions and references; eight begin with exact decisions. These findings connect intent fidelity to executable decisions: choosing a different offer must still respect the same authorization. Evaluating delegation requires examining both the selected purchase and the admitted domain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
SET makes authorization explicit before optimizing worst-case value, improving useful purchasing decisions across the tested primary configurations. Current success can nevertheless leave false permissions for unselected offers undetected. Our frozen-specification study shows that these errors affect purchasing when preferred offers become unavailable, even while viable authorized alternatives remain. Cross-template replays and original-expression checks connect the resulting actions to permissions already encoded in the specification. Where reference semantics are available, evaluation should report decision correctness, false admissions and exclusions, and their behavioral consequences under specified availability changes. These checks connect intent fidelity to executable decisions. A delegated agent may choose a different offer; doing so must still respect the same authorization.

\section*{Limitations}
The evaluated contracts are synthetic, with complete catalogues, known attributes, correct item grounding, and finite state sets. Risk limits and utility are supplied as inputs. The main benchmark isolates authorization boundaries. Its unique feasible candidate provides limited evidence about ranking among several admissible purchases. The availability study instead retains multiple viable purchases and tests a constructed, reference-stratified distribution. Its directed-removal rates describe pressure on frozen specifications, not random stockouts or deployment frequencies. Intent tests signal unresolved readings explicitly. The evaluation does not cover open-ended interpretation discovery, hidden merchant terms, unknown inventory, fraud, or settlement.

Decision conditions can omit a relevant requirement or bind it to the wrong state, even when the resulting specification executes successfully. Reference implementations check consistency with stipulated semantics and share a development workflow. Configuration, language, and diagnostic panels overlap and do not provide independent task samples. Catalogue visibility and provider compute differ across some controls. The appendix specifies the reported cohorts and evaluation protocols. Workflow gains concern the complete decision process; availability replays test frozen generated conditions. Earlier selected-output and exploratory studies remain separately archived and are not pooled with these results.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
All purchases are synthetic, with no monetary transfers or customer records. Financial consequences follow stipulated benchmark contracts. Saved artifacts distinguish original responses, reference-assisted interventions, and subsequent analysis. In deployment, erroneous acceptance could cause financial loss, while erroneous refusal could prevent access to a needed service. A deployed system would need explicit authorization and failure handling. Correct execution alone cannot establish that a compiled specification faithfully represents the user's request.
