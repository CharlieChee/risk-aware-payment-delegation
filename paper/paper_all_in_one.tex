\begin{abstract}
Payment agents may need to commit to a purchase while relevant uncertainty remains and without further user interaction. Their decisions must respect interacting requirements and financial consequences while preserving opportunities for useful completion. We study risk-aware payment delegation as a language-to-decision problem: identify one purchase that is admissible across retained interpretations or contractual events and has positive worst-case value, or refuse when none exists. We present a state-wise intent DSL and an executable framework that carry decision-relevant alternatives from natural-language requests into hard requirements, risk conditions, and utility. A language-model front end instantiates this predefined representation; a fixed evaluator checks candidates and performs selection or abstention. Reference-checked contractual boundary tasks show improvements in both safe completion and justified refusal over direct answering. Cross-model, reasoning-effort, language, and reuse studies examine the scope and cost of these gains. Controlled intent-ambiguity tests and semantic diagnostics examine decisions across unresolved readings and errors that execution cannot repair. The formulation, implementation, and controlled testbed support the study of useful payment decisions within explicit risk boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language agents increasingly turn natural-language requests into actions through tools and external services \citep{react}. Shopping, travel-planning, and service benchmarks already test grounded choices, interacting requirements, and policy-governed workflows \citep{webshop,travelplanner,taubench}. Payment delegation adds a consequential decision: committing the user's resources to a purchase whose acceptability may depend on events that have not occurred. A purchase can meet its initial budget yet expose the user to an unacceptable cancellation or replacement cost. We study this setting with no further user interaction after the initial delegation. The agent must preserve the value of the delegation while respecting the requirements under which it may act.

Relevant uncertainty concerns both the meaning of a request and the consequences of a purchase. The request and available context may support several reasonable interpretations without specifying a unique intended reading; a contract may permit several future outcomes. Our setting excludes further user interaction and does not require either source of uncertainty to be resolved before acting. Computing the joint effect of conditional clauses adds a distinct difficulty. Selecting one interpretation or event prematurely can overlook an unacceptable consequence, while refusing solely because uncertainty remains can discard a purchase acceptable under every retained possibility. The central question is therefore: \emph{can the agent identify one useful purchase that remains admissible across the retained interpretations and outcomes?}

Existing research provides complementary foundations. Uncertainty-aware planning and clarification methods determine when information should be requested \citep{knowno,intentsim,sage}. Value of Information explicitly balances the benefit of asking against interaction costs, including in flight-booking and e-commerce tasks \citep{voi}. Risk-Averse Calibration (RAC) relates uncertainty sets to consequence-sensitive action selection \citep{rac}, while program-aided and neurosymbolic reasoning separate language interpretation from computation \citep{pal,logiclm,logicpy}. These advances motivate a specific language-to-decision task: with no further user response, construct executable purchasing conditions from the request's requirements, conditional scope, and financial consequences, then determine whether those conditions permit a useful action.

Our approach retains decision-relevant possibilities and expresses what the \emph{same} purchase must satisfy in each. Hard requirements and authorized risk limits determine admissibility; utility ranks the admissible purchases. A favorable utility cannot compensate for a violated permission. Conversely, unresolved uncertainty is not itself a reason to refuse: an admissible purchase with positive guaranteed value remains actionable.

We present a \emph{state-wise executable framework} for risk-aware payment delegation under unresolved uncertainty. Its predefined representation associates decision-relevant interpretations and outcomes with state-dependent requirements, risk conditions, and utility. This carries unresolved ambiguity into purchasing: one action must remain admissible across the retained possibilities, while its value is assessed by worst-case utility. Building on RAC's fixed-set max--min principle, a fixed procedure selects a positive-value maximizing purchase or abstains when none exists. Our implementation uses a language-model front end to instantiate the representation from the request, context, and attribute schema, followed by deterministic evaluation over candidate offers.

We evaluate this approach on 200 reference-checked tasks spanning 20 contractual structures with explicit event uncertainty. Across two DeepSeek configurations, exact decisions increase from 62.0\% to 92.5\%, while unsafe acceptances and false refusals both decrease. Separate controlled tests examine retained pronoun and scope ambiguity; reference interventions trace failures to omitted interpretations. Cross-model, effort, language, and reuse studies examine reliability and cost. These complementary studies support useful decisions within explicit risk boundaries. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Risk-aware payment delegation:} a language-to-decision formulation that retains decision-relevant alternatives and separates admissibility, guaranteed value, and justified refusal without further user interaction.
\item \textbf{An executable payment-decision framework:} an inspectable state-wise interface linking retained alternatives to requirements, financial permissions, and utility, with fixed decision semantics and reusable catalogue-independent specifications.
\item \textbf{Controlled reliability evaluation:} reference-checked paired payment boundaries, cross-configuration studies, and semantic diagnostics that measure safe completion, missed feasible purchases, and remaining compilation failures.
\end{itemize}

Code, benchmark materials, retained model responses, evaluation records, and reproduction tools are publicly available in our anonymous artifact package (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).\footnote{\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/risk-aware-payment-delegation-D4FC/}}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Uncertainty and consequential decisions.}
KnowNo, IntentSim, and AmbiK examine uncertainty and clarification in planning \citep{knowno,intentsim,ambik}. VoI weighs the utility of information against user effort, while SAGE structures uncertainty over tool parameters \citep{voi,sage}. Related studies address abstention, ambiguous questions, and formal uncertainty \citep{sconu,solveuncertainty,wrongquestion,gofu}. RAC connects prediction sets to risk-averse decisions; risk-control and action-conditional extensions add statistical guarantees under their assumptions \citep{rac,crc,nonxcrc,actionrac,clm}. \citet{uncertaingeneration} apply Bayesian and risk-averse decisions to tutoring and reviewing. We use the inherited decision principle for executable purchases with explicit financial permissions, a common decision across states, and an outside option. The operating condition excludes further clarification; it does not imply that prior interaction methods cannot act under uncertainty.

\paragraph{Executable reasoning and formalization.}
PAL, Logic-LM, LINC, and Logic.py delegate computation to interpreters, provers, or solvers \citep{pal,logiclm,linc,logicpy}. Formalization studies investigate the fidelity of language-to-logic translation and its sensitivity to natural descriptions \citep{folcode,fover,planninglimit,verify}. We inherit the separation of compilation and execution, with state-dependent purchasing conditions as the generated object. Separating stable requirements from catalogue data also limits the need to repeatedly process large contexts \citep{lostmiddle}. Our evaluation measures the entire decision architecture; supplementary execution controls distinguish shared computational benefits from representation-dependent failures.

\paragraph{Constrained agents and evaluation.}
GuardAgent generates executable safeguards, DeAction corrects proposed actions, AgentSpec enforces structured runtime constraints, and VeriGuard verifies policies and actions \citep{guardagent,deaction,agentspec,veriguard}. AutoCO studies executable constraint-relaxation strategies; financial metric graphs constrain derivations \citep{autoco,financialgraph}. Our focus is the completion/refusal boundary of a purchase under retained states, including separate admissibility and value conditions. PlanBench, CoPE, FollowBench, ComplexBench, and AgentIF test planning or composed instructions \citep{planbench,cope,followbench,complexbench,agentif}; ToolEmu, AgentDojo, and AgentHarm assess consequential or adversarial agent behavior \citep{toolemu,agentdojo,agentharm}. We contribute controlled contractual boundaries and semantic diagnostics, drawing on behavioral testing and contrastive evaluation \citep{checklist,hans,contrastsets}. Authorization-domain auditing supplements decision evaluation within this task.

\section{Risk-Aware Payment Delegation: Formulation and Execution}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:contract}
The framework carries decision-relevant alternatives from a natural-language delegation into a state-wise representation, then applies predefined risk and value rules to decide whether and how to act.

\subsection{Decision Setting and Requirements}
\label{sec:formulation}
Let $x=(P,c)$ contain a natural-language request $P$ and context $c$, and let $z\in\Z$ describe a complete candidate purchase. A nonempty $\Theta(x)$ contains retained interpretations and/or relevant future events. States index conditions under which the same purchase is evaluated; a unique intended interpretation need not be identifiable. No further user interaction is available in this task. The catalogue $C\subseteq\Z$ is finite; state probabilities need not be available.

For each state, $H_\theta(x,z)$ encodes hard requirements, $\ell_\theta(x,z)$ financial consequences, $\A_\theta(x)$ the authorized risk region, and $u_\theta(x,z)$ utility on a common cardinal scale. For decision analysis, we assume that the representation preserves the request's decision-relevant interpretations, events, and associated requirements, consequences, risk permissions, and utility. Define
\begin{align}
F_\theta(x)&=\{z:H_\theta(x,z)=1,\ \ell_\theta(x,z)\in\A_\theta(x)\},\label{eq:state}\\
F(x)&=\bigcap_{\theta\in\Theta(x)}F_\theta(x),\label{eq:common}\\
\psi_x(z)&=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta(x)}u_\theta(x,z).\label{eq:robust}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:state} checks one state's requirements and consequences. Equation~\eqref{eq:common} requires one purchase to pass every such check: $\exists z\,\forall\theta$, not a different purchase after observing each state. Equation~\eqref{eq:robust} gives that purchase's lowest utility over the same states. They jointly define
\begin{equation}
\max_{z\in C\cap F(x)}\psi_x(z),
\label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
with abstention worth zero. An empty feasible domain or a nonpositive maximum requires abstention; otherwise a maximizer should be selected. Admissibility and value are separate: a high score cannot authorize an outcome outside $F(x)$.

The max--min principle for a fixed uncertainty set follows RAC \citep{rac}. Here, explicit purchasing permissions define feasibility; we implement the decision layer without conformal calibration. Context fixes risk permissions and utility before optimization. An allowance for extra spending, when specified, changes the relevant predicate rather than being inferred from infeasibility. The formulation also accommodates retained linguistic interpretations (Appendix~\ref{app:ambiguity}); the primary evaluation supplies finite contractual event families and stipulated utilities.

\subsection{State-Wise Intent DSL}
\label{sec:contracts}
\label{sec:intent-dsl}
We define a lightweight domain-specific language (intent DSL), $\Lang$, as the executable intermediate representation between a natural-language delegation and the decision procedure. Executable request languages connect semantic parsing to task execution \citep{thingtalk}; financial contract languages associate domain constructs with explicit interpretation and valuation rules \citep{composingcontracts}. Our representation carries retained interpretations and contractual outcomes into risk-aware purchase selection.

An intent specification $Q\in\Lang$ contains a nonempty collection of state-indexed clauses, abstractly
\[
Q=\big\{(\widehat H_j,\widehat R_j,\widehat u_j)\big\}_{j=1}^{m}.
\]
The predicates $\widehat H_j$ and $\widehat R_j$ encode hard requirements and admissibility of financial consequences under the same state; $\widehat u_j$ encodes its utility. Expressions use attributes from schema $\Sigma$ with fixed Boolean and numeric semantics. A risk predicate realizes the test $\ell_\theta(x,z)\in\A_\theta(x)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:state}. Conditional scope, compensation order, and utility binding belong to these expressions. A loss cap bounds an admissible consequence; it is not compensation that reduces that consequence.

Clauses retain alternative readings or circumstances under which the same purchase is evaluated. Joint admissibility does not assert that the user intended their conjunction. The evaluator fixes cross-state conjunction and minimum utility (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}); the DSL exposes their state-specific inputs for inspection. This separates generated decision conditions from the fixed selection procedure. Equivalent decompositions preserve joint admissibility and worst-case value.

A semantic front end instantiates $Q=f(x,\Sigma)$ from the request and context. Our implementation uses a language model for $f$; the language, evaluation semantics, and refusal rule remain predefined. Benchmark inputs specify event families, risk permissions, and utility definitions; the model encodes their consequences. Primary compilation excludes candidate catalogues, identifiers, and ordering. A specification can be reused when requirements, state semantics, and attribute meanings remain unchanged. Concrete syntax, serialization, and interpreter details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:interface}; they do not define the scope of the framework.

\subsection{Decision Procedure}
\label{sec:realization}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision} exactly solves the compiled instance of Equation~\eqref{eq:decision} on a complete finite catalogue once the evaluation checks below succeed. This guarantee concerns execution of $Q$; natural-language compilation errors remain part of the end-to-end evaluation. Hats distinguish compiled quantities from reference semantics: $\widehat F_C$ passes all generated hard and risk conditions, and $\widehat\psi$ is minimum generated utility. Before selection, every candidate's admissibility must be decided and every admissible candidate's state utilities known. The procedure returns all positive-value maximizers using exact integer/rational arithmetic, preserving inclusive bounds and one-subunit differences.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Compile once; evaluate risk and value}
\label{alg:decision}
\small
\textbf{Input:} request/context $x$, schema $\Sigma$, catalogue $C$\par
\textbf{Output:} \textsc{Accept}, \textsc{Refuse}, or \textsc{Fail}, with diagnostics\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}r@{\quad}p{.88\columnwidth}@{}}
1 & $Q\leftarrow f(x,\Sigma)$ \quad\emph{model compilation; no $C$}\\
2 & Decode and validate $Q$ against the interface.\\
3 & If invalid, return \textsc{Fail}(compilation).\\
4 & Evaluate every generated state on every $z\in C$; retain the condition/value matrix.\\
5 & If execution is invalid, return \textsc{Fail}(execution).\\
6 & For each $z\in C$, set $\widehat f(z)=\bigwedge_j(\widehat H_j(z)\wedge\widehat R_j(z))$.\\
7 & If catalogue coverage or any $\widehat f(z)$ is unresolved, or a candidate with $\widehat f(z)=1$ has an unknown state utility, return \textsc{Fail}(uncertified).\\
8 & $\widehat F_C\leftarrow\{z\in C:\widehat f(z)=1\}$.\\
9 & If $\widehat F_C=\varnothing$, return \textsc{Refuse}(infeasible).\\
10 & For $z\in\widehat F_C$, set $\widehat\psi(z)=\min_j\widehat u_j(z)$.\\
11 & $v\leftarrow\max_{z\in\widehat F_C}\widehat\psi(z)$.\\
12 & If $v\le0$, return \textsc{Refuse}(nonpositive value).\\
13 & Return \textsc{Accept}($\{z\in\widehat F_C:\widehat\psi(z)=v\},v$).\\
\end{tabular}\smallskip\par
\emph{Fixed evaluator: lines 2--13. Reference answers are used only in offline evaluation.}
\end{algorithm}

Unresolved evidence prevents certification of feasibility or optimality. A known feasible candidate cannot be certified optimal while another potentially feasible candidate has unknown value; known violations already exclude a candidate regardless of its utility. Primary inputs supply complete catalogues and known attributes, although undefined generated expressions can still fail evaluation. For archived executions with unresolved inputs, agreement on a certified subset does not establish Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision}'s complete-domain guarantee (Appendix~\ref{app:interface}). For $M$ candidates, $K$ generated states, and expression-evaluation cost $T$, exhaustive execution takes $O(MKT)$ work.

\subsection{Scope and Semantic Failure Modes}
\label{sec:verification}
Execution faithfully applies $Q$; preserving the request requires the generated states, permissions, and utility to be faithful as well. Omitting an event, misbinding a clause, or subtracting a risk allowance as if it were compensation can change $\widehat F_C$. Miscomputing consequences can change either feasibility or ranking. These are semantic compilation errors even when the evaluator runs successfully.

We test them offline against finite references through three complementary checks. \emph{Decision correctness} checks the selected maximizers and their guaranteed value. \emph{Authorization-domain correctness} checks $\widehat F_C\subseteq C\cap F(x)$, including unselected candidates. \emph{Refusal correctness} checks that no reference-admissible candidate has positive guaranteed value. A safe current selection can mask an unsafe admitted alternative, while an overly restrictive rule can cause a false refusal. These diagnostics characterize the method's failures; they are not an additional runtime oracle. Further denotation properties and assumptions appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

The evaluation concerns executable meaning rather than identical expression strings. Equivalent state decompositions can produce the same admissibility and minimum utility. Conversely, syntactically valid expressions with plausible labels can encode a different decision problem. The finite task therefore makes semantic comparisons possible through candidate-level conditions and values, independently of the surface representation.

When requirements, state functions, permissions, and attribute meanings remain fixed, $Q$ can be reused on another catalogue. Our reuse study evaluates this operational property, and frozen-rule replays examine whether a change in availability exposes a latent semantic error (Sections~\ref{sec:permissions} and~\ref{sec:reuse}).\par


\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Controlled payment boundaries.}
We construct 200 tasks from 20 contract templates in four families: coupon cancellation, insured cancellation, threshold-dependent returns, and service replacement. Contracts combine overlapping groups, compensation layers, shipping charges, and item/order caps. Each purchase has six components. The event universe contains all affected subsets of size at most four, including the empty event: 57 states per candidate. Catalogues contain 128--1,024 candidates. All amounts use abstract monetary units (MU), with 100 integer subunits per MU. Exact arithmetic on subunits preserves strict and inclusive financial boundaries.



Each template contributes five feasible/infeasible pairs. A feasible task has exactly one authorized positive-value candidate. Changing only its administrative fee moves its worst loss one subunit above the limit; other attributes and offer order stay fixed. These 100 pairs test boundary adherence and solution finding \citep{checklist,contrastsets,hans}. Separate cash-ledger and expression implementations agree on 6,712,320 candidate--event checks, supplemented by 25,600 random-attribute checks and three hand-calculated witnesses. Both implementations arose within one development workflow. Inputs and references were frozen before primary calls (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
The \direct--\ours\ comparison evaluates the complete executable decision architecture. \direct\ receives the request, schema, full catalogue, and robust decision rules; it returns a decision, selected identifiers, and guaranteed utility. \ours\ compiles a catalogue-independent rule for the fixed evaluator under the same decision obligations. Procedural execution controls, including a matched-input comparison, examine shared computational gains and realization-dependent outcomes (Appendix~\ref{app:execution-controls}, Table~\ref{tab:execution-ablation}).

The primary comparison spans eleven model configurations: Astra max; Sol, Terra, and Luna medium; DeepSeek low and high; Qwen Flash and Max low; GLM Flash low and GLM high; and Opus 5.5 medium. Cohort definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:primary-extensions}; other panels retain their own cohorts.

\paragraph{Outcome scoring.}
An \emph{exact decision} returns the reference decision, authorized maximizing choices, and correct utility. \emph{Safe task completion} is exact acceptance on a feasible task. \emph{Unsafe acceptance} returns a reference-infeasible choice; \emph{false refusal} rejects an available positive-value authorized purchase. Other groups remaining value and execution failures. Execution failures are never credited as correct refusals; all failures remain in each completed cohort's denominator (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Authorization auditing also checks unselected admitted candidates. Archived trials retain one semantic attempt, bounded transport retries, responses, usage, duration, and evaluation. Syntax recovery preserves expression meaning. Comparisons are paired within configurations; service-specific compute budgets are not equivalent.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The anonymous artifact provides code, benchmark inputs and references, prompts, configurations, retained responses, and evaluation records. An offline entry reconstructs tables and the four main figures from frozen reporting inputs, checks paired outcome partitions and Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision}, and records input hashes without model calls. Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction} details the material coverage and workflow; Table~\ref{tab:configurations} specifies generation settings.

\subsection{Safe Completion under Contractual Uncertainty}
\label{sec:boundaries}



Figure~\ref{fig:completion} combines model coverage, joint safety--completion outcomes, and contract-level variation. All eleven configurations improve exactness, reduce unsafe acceptances, and complete more authorized purchases. Across the two primary DeepSeek configurations, exact decisions rise from 248/400 (62.0\%) to 370/400 (92.5\%). The gain is 30.5 percentage points, with a 95\% template-cluster bootstrap interval of $[22.75,37.75]$, retaining both settings within each template. Exact purchases rise from 100 to 186 across 200 feasible outcomes, while unsafe acceptances fall from 79 to 9 and false refusals from 53 to 1. Safety improves together with useful completion.

Each paired task changes only one fee and requires the opposite decision. Joint metrics test responsiveness to financial conditions, beyond blanket acceptance or refusal.

The updated Astra max comparison scores 180/200 (90\%) for direct answering and 196/200 (98\%) for \ours. Direct has six unsafe acceptances, six false refusals, and eight Other failures; \ours\ has two unsafe acceptances and two false refusals. Traced errors from the original archived run illustrate both boundary failures (Appendix~\ref{app:witnesses}).

\label{sec:components}



We replay 648 frozen DeepSeek artifacts from 72 tasks and three effort settings, using Set and procedural controls with matched or visible catalogue input (Appendix~\ref{app:permissionaudit}). \section{Conclusion}
We study payment delegation without further user interaction while uncertainty remains. An inspectable state-wise intent interface connects purchasing requirements, financial permissions, and utility to a fixed decision procedure. On the primary boundary benchmark, this executable framework improves authorization adherence and useful completion over direct answering. Retained-intent tests, semantic diagnostics, and cross-configuration studies examine these decisions; catalogue reuse reduces repeated model calls. Together, the formulation, implementation, and testbed support useful payment decisions within explicit risk boundaries.
\label{mainend}

\section*{Limitations}
\paragraph{Semantic and decision scope.}
The experiments use synthetic purchasing contracts, complete catalogues, correct item grounding, known attributes, and finite uncertainty sets. The primary benchmark concerns contractual events; the historical intent panel supplies explicit cues to unresolved pronouns and scope. Open-ended discovery of plausible interpretations remains to be evaluated. Preferences and risk boundaries are specified rather than learned. Faithfully instantiating ambiguity and contractual semantics remains a limitation of the current front end. End-to-end evaluations retain representation errors. Conformal coverage would require additional calibration. Actual settlement, hidden merchant terms, fraud, and incomplete inventory are outside the tested task.

\paragraph{Evaluation and implementation scope.}
The primary benchmark deliberately pairs numerical boundaries with one feasible candidate or none. It supports controlled boundary comparisons, not deployment error-rate estimates or broad ranking over many feasible purchases. The references are separate implementations within one development workflow. Historical diagnostics, effort settings, translations, and replays share cases and are reported as distinct cohorts. Catalogue visibility and service compute budgets vary by architecture or provider; matched-input and execution controls are retained in the appendix. Some generated specifications remain incorrect or invalid. Primary effects concern the full compilation-and-execution pipeline; Appendix~\ref{app:execution-controls} examines realization-dependent outcomes. Operational failures remain in their designated denominators.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
All purchases are synthetic, with no monetary transfers or customer records. Financial consequences follow stipulated benchmark contracts. Saved artifacts distinguish original responses, reference-assisted interventions, and subsequent analysis.

\section{Formalization and Executable Interface}
\label{app:proofs}
\subsection{Decision semantics and assumptions}
RAC supplies the fixed-set risk-averse decision principle \citep{rac}. Our decision analysis fixes $x=(P,c)$, a nonempty retained state set $\Theta(x)$, and state-dependent hard requirements, risk permissions, and utilities before optimization. Utilities share a cardinal scale and are bounded over the finite experimental domain. The representation is assumed to preserve decision-relevant interpretations and their associated conditions and consequences. This does not require a uniquely resolved intent, a distribution over states, linearity, or convexity. No conformal coverage or action-conditional calibration is implemented.

\paragraph{One action across readings.}
\label{app:ambiguity}
For ``Buy tickets for Wang and Li; his ticket must be refundable,'' retain $\theta_W$ and $\theta_L$. One candidate $z$ specifies both tickets. Membership in $F_{\theta_W}(x)\cap F_{\theta_L}(x)$ requires both refundability conditions and the shared budget. Buying two refundable tickets is compatible with each reading; it does not assert that the user explicitly requested both to be refundable. A common positive-value purchase can be selected without resolving the referent or asking the user. Controlled evaluations of this behavior are in Appendix~\ref{app:intent}.

\subsection{Representation and evaluation}
\label{app:interface}
Each state record contains a label and expressions \texttt{hard}, \texttt{risk}, and \texttt{utility}. Boolean expressions support conjunction, disjunction, negation, comparisons, and conditionals. Arithmetic supports exact literals, basic operations, minimum, maximum, and absolute value. Boolean and numeric values have distinct types. The attribute schema grounds expression variables; an unavailable applicable attribute cannot certify feasibility. The primary benchmark provides complete attributes.

For generated states $j=1,\ldots,m$, evaluation gives
\begin{align}
\widehat f(z)&=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}(\widehat H_j(z)\wedge\widehat R_j(z)),\\
\widehat\psi(z)&=\min_{1\le j\le m}\widehat u_j(z).
\end{align}
The fixed procedure first requires complete enumeration of $C$, a decided $\widehat f(z)$ for every candidate, and known state utilities whenever $\widehat f(z)=1$. An aggregate predicate with a known false conjunct is false even if another conjunct is unknown. Failure to establish these conditions produces \textsc{Fail}(uncertified), before any purchase is selected. The procedure then forms $\widehat F_C=\{z\in C:\widehat f(z)=1\}$ and maximizes $\widehat\psi$ over that domain. Any nonempty subset of tied positive-value maximizers is valid. An empty domain or nonpositive maximum produces refusal. Invalid expressions produce an interface/execution failure, not a certified refusal. State labels alone do not affect the decision; equivalent merged expressions may encode the same functions.

\paragraph{Exactness on the declared domain.}
After these checks, membership in $\widehat F_C$ is decided for every $z\in C$, and all values over that domain are known. Finite enumeration therefore returns the complete set of maximizers when the maximum is positive. Otherwise, an empty domain or a nonpositive maximum proves that the compiled problem has no positive-value option. No unresolved candidate is silently excluded from the optimization.

For unresolved inputs, the historical experiment executor operates only on the certified observed subset. Such outputs fall outside Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision}'s exact-solution contract. Recorded outcome accuracy compares returned actions and values with the reference; a matching no-action outcome does not by itself certify infeasibility. Runtime reasons distinguish insufficient evidence from an established negative result.

These hatted functions are instantiated by the model. The reference $F$ and $\psi$ enter offline scoring, never runtime selection. Execution is exact for the generated expressions; correctness relative to the request additionally requires faithful state semantics.

\paragraph{Concrete syntax and serialization.}
The implementation serializes intent-DSL instances as JSON, a carrier for the specification. Each state record contains a label and expressions for hard requirements, risk admissibility, and utility; their meaning comes from the expression language and evaluation rules. An illustrative record is:
\begin{quote}\small
\begin{verbatim}
{"label": "service_failure",
 "hard": "price <= 100000",
 "risk": "max(0,loss-cover) <= 20000",
 "utility":
   "110000-price-max(0,loss-cover)"}
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}
Here the schema defines price, gross loss, and compensation in integer monetary subunits. This record encodes the failure-state conditions in Table~\ref{tab:trace}; a full specification includes the nonempty state list and runtime metadata. It is an interface illustration, not an observed model output. State-indexed semantics, rather than JSON keys alone, define the intent DSL.

\paragraph{Carrier decoding and bounds.}
A shared schema-aware decoder may recover a uniquely identifiable schema-valid JSON object without consulting reference answers; it does not edit expression meaning. The expression engine permits 200,000 characters per expression, 100,000 AST nodes, depth 100, 256 states, 10,000 candidates, and one million state--candidate combinations. All primary problems are expressible within these bounds. The Python control allows loops, functions, conditionals, \texttt{math}, \texttt{itertools}, and exact fractions, with four CPU seconds/eight wall seconds per catalogue and a one-MiB output bound. A reference program, withheld from models, verifies that the largest primary catalogue fits this execution budget.

\subsection{Output contracts and finite realization}
For completeness, distinguish a compiled model $Q$, whose pre-selection domain is $\widehat F_C$, from an output contract $Q_{\mathrm{out}}$, whose denotation $S_x(Q_{\mathrm{out}})\subseteq\Z$ contains its permitted purchases. Relative to reference semantics, an accepted contract satisfies
\begin{equation}
\varnothing\ne S_x(Q_{\mathrm{out}})\subseteq F(x).
\label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
Its guaranteed value is
\begin{equation}
W_x(Q_{\mathrm{out}})=\inf_{z\in S_x(Q_{\mathrm{out}})}\psi_x(z).
\label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
For a promised value $t>0$, feasibility and $W_x\ge t$ are equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\varnothing\ne S_x(Q_{\mathrm{out}})\subseteq K_t(x),\\
&K_t(x)=\{z\in F(x):\psi_x(z)\ge t\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
Indeed, membership gives feasibility and the pointwise bound $\psi_x(z)\ge t$; conversely, a lower bound on the infimum bounds every permitted purchase. These are reference obligations, not an extra runtime oracle. Restricting a sound outcome set preserves its requirements; deleting a predicate may instead enlarge the domain.

\begin{proposition}[Finite contract realization]
\label{prop:finite}
For finite $C$, exact evaluation, bounded utility, and a language denoting every singleton in $C$, if $F_C=F(x)\cap C\ne\varnothing$, the best guaranteed value of a nonempty admissible contract in $C$ equals $\max_{z\in F_C}\psi_x(z)$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} A maximizer $z^*$ exists on finite $F_C$. Every nonempty $S\subseteq F_C$ has $\inf_{z\in S}\psi_x(z)\le\psi_x(z^*)$; a contract denoting $\{z^*\}$ attains equality. Acceptance improves on the zero outside option exactly when this maximum is positive. If $F_C$ is empty, no nonempty admissible contract exists.\hfill$\square$

This conditional expressibility result neither proves that generated predicates are faithful nor gives a statistical guarantee. Context fixes authorization; infeasibility cannot create permission for extra spending. Reuse requires unchanged attribute meanings, requirements, state semantics, and authorization. That property is shared by executable realizations, including Python.

\subsection{Common instructions and outcome protocol}
\label{app:implementation}
All primary routes receive the same substantive policy, summarized below. Frozen model-facing prompts retain their original language; this English rendering describes the policy rather than replacing those prompts.
\begin{quote}\small
Retain applicable interpretations. Respect current authorization in every retained state. Evaluate hard requirements and the complete risk permission, then maximize the minimum state-dependent utility. Buy only for positive guaranteed value; otherwise refuse. Do not ask the user or change permissions.
\end{quote}
Direct returns a decision, allowed identifiers, and guaranteed utility. Set returns the state-wise expressions of Appendix~\ref{app:interface}. The supplementary procedural control is defined in Appendix~\ref{app:execution-controls}. Exactness requires the reference decision, authorized maximizing purchases, and correct value. Disjoint failures are unsafe acceptance, false refusal, and Other (remaining value or execution failures). Execution failure is never credited as correct refusal.

\section{Controlled Decisions under Retained Intent Ambiguity}
\label{app:intent}
\subsection{Task semantics and controlled inputs}
The frozen parameter-boundary campaign contains three families with unresolved user interpretations. We include all three, selecting by task semantics; five other families with conditional but unambiguous requirements are excluded. This retrospective study is separate from the contractual-event primary benchmark.

\paragraph{Ticket-holder reference.}
Two male travellers need tickets; ``his'' ticket must be refundable with a fee below the authorized limit. Utility penalizes that same traveller's delay and refund fee. Both referents remain possible, linking each interpretation's hard requirement, risk bound, and value function.

\paragraph{Hotel reference and conditional binding.}
Two guests need correctly registered rooms. ``His room'' must permit cancellation at 18:00 if he arrives at or after 18:00, or 24 hours before his expected arrival otherwise. The cancellation fee and utility weights refer to the same guest, so the unresolved antecedent affects conditional requirements and value.

\paragraph{Distributive versus collective scope.}
Gifts must satisfy recipients' preferences and wrapping requirements. ``Buy one for each; the exchange top-up is at most $r$'' may bound each recipient or the batch. Stipulated utility uses the corresponding maximum individual or total top-up. With nonnegative charges the batch bound may dominate; the correct bound and score still need to be retained.

All requests explicitly signal unresolved reference or scope. Methods see identical user text, complete catalogue attributes, synthetic utility formulas, and the common decision policy. These historical compilation calls see the catalogue, unlike the primary Set protocol. No reference expressions, answer, or subsequent user clarification is supplied. The study tests decisions with retained meanings, not automatic discovery of unrestricted ambiguity.

\subsection{Omitted-interpretation and component interventions}
\label{app:oracle}
The source pool contains 56 Qwen/GLM Set generations for one ambiguous ticket request and two catalogues (128 and 512 offers). Sixteen outputs admit a reference-unsafe candidate and are selected for diagnosis. Each omits the reading in which ``his'' refers to Li, retaining only Zhang's fee-free-refund conditions and delay-weighted utility. These are 16 faulty outputs, not 16 independent requests. Restoring Li's state restores its conditions and utility; duplicating Zhang's label cannot do so.



The original rules select safe purchases but have incorrect values and admitted domains. Reference admissibility alone repairs the domain; reference utility alone makes the unsafe offer win. Both components, or the correctly restored state, recover exact decisions in all 16 selected outputs (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}). These offline interventions diagnose semantic content and are not an inference-time discovery or repair algorithm.

\subsection{Input intervention on the same request}
\label{app:intent-input}
Using the 512-offer request, six configurations, three input conditions, and two samples produces 36 calls, with 12 generations per condition. Configurations are Qwen Max off/medium, Qwen Flash low, GLM Flash low, GLM low, and Astra max. The reference-interpretation condition explicitly preserves both possible referents and the corresponding utility binding, without giving a selected offer or reference formula. It is researcher-supplied input, not an additional user response.



Catalogue withholding alone does not remove the errors. Supplied interpretations yield 11/12 exact decisions and one Other failure; no condition selects an unsafe offer on the original catalogue. This local intervention identifies a representation dependency rather than a population-level success rate.

\subsection{Two primary decision examples}
\label{app:witnesses}
Reference evaluation checks all 57 events. In one Astra Direct return-cost case, the reported loss of 1,954.33~MU understates the contractual loss of 2,054.33~MU against a 2,000~MU limit; no offer is feasible. In a coupon-cancellation case, Direct rejects all 1,024 offers although one has positive value and loss of 1,999.99~MU. These primary examples illustrate unsafe acceptance and false refusal at the same authorization boundary.
