\begin{abstract}
Delegated purchasing must respect existing authorization when interpretations or contractual outcomes remain uncertain. We formulate purchase or refusal without further user interaction by separating joint authorization from worst-case utility. SET selects a purchase admissible across retained states with the highest positive worst-case value, or refuses. On synthetic contractual-boundary tasks across model families, it improves exact decisions and useful completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals relative to direct decisions under the same policy. Yet an unauthorized alternative may be admitted but lose in ranking. A prospective study freezes decision conditions and varies candidate availability: removing a few high-ranked offers exposes unauthorized purchases across contract families, even while alternatives remain authorized and positively valued under both the specification and the reference. One specification starts from an exact decision but purchases an unauthorized offer after four removals, with 59 viable authorized alternatives remaining. Separate reference-assisted interventions on selected faulty outputs show how correcting utility can also expose overbroad permissions. These analyses distinguish correctness of the selected purchase from soundness of the admitted domain: losing a preferred offer does not expand the user's authorization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Language agents use external tools to carry out multi-step tasks \citep{react,schick2023toolformer}. Benchmarks cover shopping, travel, and service workflows \citep{webshop,travelplanner,taubench}, as well as broader web tasks \citep{zhou2024webarena,deng2023mind2web}. ST-WebAgentBench counts completion as successful only when applicable policies are satisfied \citep{stwebagentbench}. In delegated purchasing, the commitment and its possible consequences must stay within the user's authorization. Cancellation, return, or replacement costs can exceed that authorization even when the initial price is within budget.

Requests can have several readings, and future events determine financial consequences. We study purchase or refusal without another user turn, using only existing authorization. Choosing a convenient reading can permit an unauthorized purchase; refusing every uncertain request can discard useful options. Yet one purchase may satisfy every retained possibility. The task is to find a common authorized purchase with positive worst-case value.

Clarification and uncertainty-aware planning use additional information to improve decisions \citep{knowno,voi,sage}. We apply the fixed-set max--min principle of Risk-Averse Calibration (RAC) \citep{rac}, with purchasing permissions that cannot be traded against utility. Turning intent into executable decisions requires preserving which conditions apply under each reading or event. For example, a loss limit restricts permission; it is not compensation that reduces the loss. That distinction affects both admissibility and value.

We introduce \ours\ to evaluate authorization across retained states and rank admissible purchases by worst-case utility through a fixed procedure. We assess how reliably this workflow turns natural-language delegation into purchasing decisions across model families.

A preferred offer becoming unavailable does not expand the user's authorization. Yet a safe purchase can reflect either a rule that excludes unauthorized offers or a ranking that keeps them from winning. In one controlled replay, a specification initially makes an exact decision, but selects an unauthorized offer after four high-ranked offers are removed, while 59 alternatives remain permitted and positively valued under both its conditions and the reference. The request, permissions, utilities, and surviving offers are unchanged. We analyze how availability and valuation separately expose existing permission errors, and why a correct current purchase cannot certify the entire admitted domain.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Risk-aware decisions under unresolved uncertainty:} a formulation of purchase or refusal that requires a common authorized action and separates its permission from its worst-case value.
\item \textbf{Authorization errors hidden by selection:} we distinguish selected-action correctness from domain soundness, derive the effects of state omission, and explain when availability or valuation exposes an existing permission error.
\item \textbf{Controlled empirical investigation:} multi-model boundary and intent tests assess decision reliability; a prospective availability study retains viable authorized alternatives, and separate component interventions trace permission--value interactions.
\end{itemize}

Code, benchmark materials, retained responses, evaluation records, and reproduction tools are available in our anonymous artifact package (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).\footnote{\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/risk-aware-payment-delegation-D4FC/}}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Decisions under uncertainty.}
KnowNo and AmbiK address ambiguous planning instructions \citep{knowno,ambik}; IntentSim evaluates clarification across language tasks \citep{intentsim}. AmbigQA and AmbiEnt examine multiple readings in question answering and natural language inference \citep{min2020ambigqa,liu2023ambiguity}. VoI weighs information against user effort, and SAGE represents uncertainty over tool parameters \citep{voi,sage}. We study purchase or refusal without another user turn. RAC supplies a max--min rule over prediction sets; related methods study calibrated risk guarantees \citep{rac,crc,actionrac}. We apply the fixed-set principle to purchasing permissions and a common action across states, with refusal as the outside option. Our analysis does not add a calibration guarantee.

\paragraph{Policy reasoning and decision conditions.}
Complex-policy studies identify workflow-governing rules as a major reasoning difficulty \citep{complexpolicy}. AgentIF examines conditional constraints and rules governing which constraints apply and take priority \citep{agentif}. These findings motivate attention to relationships among conditions. Executable reasoning separates interpretation from computation \citep{pal,logiclm,linc}; semantic parsing captures compositional requests and constrains their representation \citep{gupta2018hierarchical,shin2021constrained}. Genie maps assistant commands to ThingTalk programs \citep{campagna2019genie}, while financial contract languages define interpretation and valuation rules \citep{composingcontracts}. Formalization studies examine translation errors \citep{folcode,fover,verify}. We study their consequences for authorization and worst-case value under retained readings and events.

\paragraph{Agent safeguards and policy compliance.}
ToolEmu and ToolSword evaluate risks in tool use \citep{toolemu,ye2024toolsword}; AgentDojo and AgentHarm study prompt injection and harmful tasks \citep{agentdojo,agentharm}. GuardAgent, DeAction, AgentSpec, and VeriGuard generate or enforce behavioral constraints \citep{guardagent,deaction,agentspec,veriguard}. AgentAuditor assesses evaluator reliability, using strict and lenient standards for ambiguous risk judgments \citep{agentauditor}. Our experiments use explicit purchasing permissions and assess both unauthorized acceptance and missed authorized purchases.

\paragraph{Errors hidden by outcomes.}
OpenAgentSafety distinguishes safe behavior from unsuccessful unsafe attempts and failures before exposure to risk \citep{openagentsafety}. Near-Miss, published at the GEM workshop, detects missing information checks before state-changing actions even when the final outcome is correct \citep{nearmiss}. These studies complement challenge sets, behavioral tests, and contrast sets \citep{mccoy2019right,checklist,contrastsets}. We examine permissions already present in a purchase specification but hidden by its selected action. Frozen-specification tests vary availability while viable authorized alternatives remain; separate component interventions examine valuation. Both connect behavior to the admitted domain, whose soundness cannot be inferred from current decision correctness.

\section{Risk-Aware Delegation under Uncertainty}
\label{sec:method}
\label{sec:contract}
We define which purchases a delegation permits before deciding which is most valuable. The same commitment must remain acceptable across the possibilities retained from the request and its contractual context.

\subsection{Authorization and Value across States}
\label{sec:formulation}
Let $P$ be the natural-language request, $c$ its context, and $x=(P,c)$. Let $\Z$ be the space of complete purchases, $C\subseteq\Z$ a finite catalogue, and $z\in C$ a candidate purchase. The nonempty state set $\Theta(x)$ contains retained interpretations, future events, or their combinations. Interpretations determine requirements; events determine purchase consequences. The same purchase is evaluated in every state without further user interaction or state probabilities.

For $\theta\in\Theta(x)$, let $H_\theta(x,z)$ encode hard requirements, $\ell_\theta(x,z)$ financial consequences, $\A_\theta(x)$ the authorized risk region, and $u_\theta(x,z)$ utility on a common cardinal scale. Define
\begin{align}
F_\theta(x)&=\{z:H_\theta(x,z)=1,\ \ell_\theta(x,z)\in\A_\theta(x)\},\label{eq:state}\\
F(x)&=\bigcap_{\theta\in\Theta(x)}F_\theta(x),\label{eq:common}\\
\psi_x(z)&=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta(x)}u_\theta(x,z).\label{eq:robust}
\end{align}
Authorization requires one purchase to satisfy every retained state: $\exists z\,\forall\theta$. Separate purchases that work under separate readings do not meet this requirement. With refusal worth zero, the decision solves
\begin{equation}
\max_{z\in C\cap F(x)}\psi_x(z).
\label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
The agent selects a maximizer only if this maximum is positive. It refuses when the domain is empty or the maximum is nonpositive. Utility ranks authorized purchases; it cannot justify violating a hard requirement or increasing a financial permission. This instantiates RAC's fixed-set max--min principle \citep{rac} with explicit authorization and an outside option.

\paragraph{A common purchase need not resolve meaning.}
In our ticket tasks, Zhang and Li need tickets within 3,000 monetary units (MU), and ``his ticket'' must allow a fee-free refund. Two 2,400-MU orders refunding only one traveller each are separately feasible but provide no common purchase. Adding a 2,800-MU order refunding both permits action, with stipulated utility $(3000-2800)/1000=1/5$. The ambiguity remains. Buying the common order relies on the existing budget and value conditions, not additional authorization (Appendix~\ref{app:observed-trace}).

These equations define the target semantics used for offline references. Experimental inputs state events, permissions, and utility conventions, but do not supply their executable reference expressions. Errors in deriving the conditions, including omitted readings and incorrect clause binding, remain part of the end-to-end evaluation.

\subsection{Purchase, Refusal, and Incomplete Evaluation}
\label{sec:realization}
\label{sec:contracts}
\label{sec:intent-dsl}
The preparation procedure $f$ maps $x$ and attribute schema $\Sigma$ (names and types) to executable conditions $Q$. A valid $Q$ has $K\ge1$ clauses, indexed by $j=1,\ldots,K$, each specifying a hard predicate $\widehat H_j$, financial-permission predicate $\widehat R_j$, and utility $\widehat u_j$. Hats denote quantities computed from $Q$ for fixed $x$. Predicate conjunction defines $\widehat F_C$, the admitted catalogue domain; minimum utility gives $\widehat\psi$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision} performs selection.

The procedure exactly solves the compiled version of Equation~\eqref{eq:decision} on a complete, resolved finite catalogue. Every candidate's admissibility, and every admitted candidate's state utilities, must be known. A feasible offer cannot be certified optimal while a competitor has unknown value. Correctness relative to the request additionally requires the decision conditions to preserve its target semantics.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Risk-aware purchase selection}
\label{alg:decision}
\small
\textbf{Input:} request/context $x$, attribute schema $\Sigma$, catalogue $C$\par
\textbf{Output:} \textsc{Accept}, \textsc{Refuse}, or \textsc{Fail}, with diagnostics\par\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}r@{\quad}p{.88\columnwidth}@{}}
1 & $Q\leftarrow f(x,\Sigma)$ \quad\emph{derive conditions; no $C$}\\
2 & Validate the generated conditions and their types.\\
3 & If invalid, return \textsc{Fail}(compilation).\\
4 & Evaluate every generated state on every $z\in C$; retain the condition/value matrix.\\
5 & If execution is invalid, return \textsc{Fail}(execution).\\
6 & For each $z\in C$, set $\widehat f(z)=\bigwedge_j(\widehat H_j(z)\wedge\widehat R_j(z))$.\\
7 & If catalogue coverage or any $\widehat f(z)$ is unresolved, or a candidate with $\widehat f(z)=1$ has an unknown state utility, return \textsc{Fail}(uncertified).\\
8 & $\widehat F_C\leftarrow\{z\in C:\widehat f(z)=1\}$.\\
9 & If $\widehat F_C=\varnothing$, return \textsc{Refuse}(infeasible).\\
10 & For $z\in\widehat F_C$, set $\widehat\psi(z)=\min_j\widehat u_j(z)$.\\
11 & $v\leftarrow\max_{z\in\widehat F_C}\widehat\psi(z)$.\\
12 & If $v\le0$, return \textsc{Refuse}(nonpositive value).\\
13 & Return \textsc{Accept}($\{z\in\widehat F_C:\widehat\psi(z)=v\},v$).\\
\end{tabular}\smallskip\par
\emph{Fixed evaluator: lines 2--13. Reference answers are used only in offline evaluation.}
\end{algorithm}

\textsc{Refuse} establishes that the evaluated conditions admit no positive-value solution. \textsc{Fail} means the required compilation or evaluation did not complete. Both prevent purchase, but only the former is a decision about the evaluated domain. The procedure computes all maximizers; the response may name a nonempty subset of tied optima with their correct value, from which one complete purchase is chosen. Exhaustive evaluation costs $O(MKT)$ for $M$ candidates, $K$ generated states, and expression cost $T$. Appendix~\ref{app:interface} gives the execution rules and guarantee conditions.

\section{Why Safe Choices Can Hide Authorization Errors}
\label{sec:analysis}
\label{sec:verification}
A safe purchase does not show whether a rule excludes unauthorized offers or merely ranks them lower. Executable delegation requires permissions that hold independently of ranking. We distinguish that requirement from correctness of the selected action.

\subsection{Selected Actions and Admitted Domains}
Write $F_C=C\cap F(x)$ for the reference-authorized domain. A selected purchase $z^\star$ is safe when $z^\star\in F_C$. Soundness of the generated authorization domain is stronger:
\begin{equation}
\widehat F_C\subseteq F_C.
\label{eq:domain-soundness}
\end{equation}
The difference $\widehat F_C\setminus F_C$ contains unauthorized offers the rule admits, including those never selected. An exact acceptance selects a reference maximizer and reports its value correctly. Even this stronger outcome check does not establish that the difference is empty. It checks the chosen purchase, leaving permissions for unselected offers untested.

The reverse difference, $F_C\setminus\widehat F_C$, contains authorized offers the rule excludes, potentially removing the best purchase or causing false refusal. An empty domain is sound but can miss an authorized task. Refusal is correct only when no reference-authorized purchase has positive guaranteed value. We therefore evaluate useful completion alongside authorization soundness.

\subsection{Omitted States Affect Permission and Value}
Fix $x$ and write $\Theta=\Theta(x)$. For a nonempty $S\subseteq\Theta$, let $F_S$ and $\psi_S$ denote the intersection in Equation~\eqref{eq:common} and the worst-case utility in Equation~\eqref{eq:robust}, restricted to states in $S$. Retaining only $\Theta'\subseteq\Theta$, with $\Theta'\ne\varnothing$ and all remaining state functions unchanged, gives
\begin{equation}
F_\Theta\subseteq F_{\Theta'},\qquad
\psi_{\Theta'}(z)\ge\psi_\Theta(z).
\label{eq:omission}
\end{equation}
Removing conjuncts cannot shrink the admissible set, and removing terms cannot lower the worst-case value. A single omission can thus weaken authorization and inflate the value used for selection. Both inequalities can be equalities: a redundant state changes neither quantity. The relevant issue is whether its conditions affect the aggregate, not the number of labels emitted. Equivalent merged conditions preserve the decision semantics.

In the hotel tasks, ``his room'' binds one guest to the arrival-dependent cancellation deadline, fee checked against the cap, and utility penalty. Omitting that reading can remove both a permission check and a source of low value. Incorrect binding is a separate error; Equation~\eqref{eq:omission} assumes deletion with all remaining functions fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:intent} gives the task semantics.

\subsection{Availability and Ranking Can Mask Permission Errors}
For a fixed request and fully evaluated specification, restricting $C$ to $C'\subseteq C$ leaves surviving offers' permissions and utilities unchanged: $\widehat F_{C'}=\widehat F_C\cap C'$ and $F_{C'}=F_C\cap C'$. Thus a sound domain remains sound under deletion. Availability can expose an existing false permission, not create one.

Suppose the only admitted offers are reference-authorized $a,c$ and unauthorized $b$, with $\psi_x(c)>0$ and
\begin{equation}
\widehat\psi(a)>\widehat\psi(b)>\widehat\psi(c)>0.
\label{eq:masking}
\end{equation}
The selector chooses $a$. Removing it exposes $b$, although $c$ remains permitted and positively valued under both the specification and reference. The failure does not require losing all viable authorized choices. An incorrectly admitted offer with nonpositive estimated value, however, cannot win under deletion alone. Domain errors and unsafe purchases therefore need separate measurements.

Under a sound domain, changing utility cannot produce an unauthorized selection either. On the unchanged three-offer catalogue, replacing $\widehat\psi$ by reference utility selects $b$ if $\psi_x(b)>\max\{0,\psi_x(a),\psi_x(c)\}$. An unauthorized purchase can have positive task value. Correct valuation does not repair an overbroad domain.

Equation~\eqref{eq:omission} explains how this masking can arise. Omitting a state may inflate the safe offer's value more than the unauthorized offer's, enough to reverse their reference ranking. Restoring utility then removes the safe offer's artificial advantage while leaving permission overbroad. A single omission can thus produce two interacting errors. Conversely, correcting permission excludes unauthorized offers but can leave wrong values among the remaining offers. Restoring the omitted state's conditions and utilities addresses both effects of the omission.

State omission is one source of the mismatch. Incorrect clause aggregation or consequence calculations can also corrupt the admitted domain. Section~\ref{sec:permissions} tests availability on a prospective cohort of frozen specifications and valuation through separate reference-assisted interventions. The availability mechanism depends on the resulting permissions and ranking, not a particular source of error.

\subsection{Tasks, Comparisons, and Scoring}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Contractual boundaries.}
We construct 200 tasks from 20 templates in four families: coupon cancellation, insured cancellation, threshold-dependent returns, and service replacement. Contracts combine overlapping groups, compensation layers, shipping charges, and item/order caps. Each purchase has six components; all subsets of up to four affected components give 57 events, including the empty event. Catalogues contain 128--1,024 candidates. One MU equals 100 integer subunits.

Each template contributes five feasible/infeasible pairs. A feasible task has exactly one authorized positive-value candidate. Increasing its fee by the feasible margin plus one subunit puts its worst loss exactly one subunit above the limit; other attributes and offer order remain fixed. The pairs isolate responsiveness to authorization boundaries \citep{checklist,contrastsets}. Separate cash-ledger and expression implementations agree on 6,712,320 candidate--event checks, supplemented by 25,600 random-attribute checks and three hand-calculated witnesses. Inputs and references were frozen before primary calls (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\paragraph{Decision workflows.}
\direct\ receives the request, schema, catalogue, and the joint-feasibility, worst-value, and refusal rules of Section~\ref{sec:method}. It returns a decision and utility without external execution. \ours\ separates preparation of the state-dependent conditions from fixed catalogue evaluation. The preparation stage receives the request and schema but excludes the catalogue. Conditions use exact arithmetic and retain clause scope, compensation order, and value binding. Evaluation semantics and observed compilations are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The shared decision policy fixes the target behavior for both methods; the comparison measures how reliably each complete workflow realizes that behavior. Appendix~\ref{app:execution} reports the textual-checklist baseline and sensitivity analyses.

\paragraph{Outcomes.}
An \emph{exact decision} agrees with the reference and, for acceptance, returns authorized maximizers with correct utility. \emph{Safe completion} is exact acceptance on a feasible task. \emph{Unsafe acceptance} selects a reference-infeasible offer; \emph{false refusal} rejects an available authorized positive-value purchase. \emph{Other} includes value errors and failed or unresolved execution, never credited as correct refusal. All outcomes remain in cohort denominators. Workflow comparisons are paired within configurations, with one semantic attempt and bounded transport retries (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). Inputs and evaluation records accompany the artifact (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).

\subsection{Useful Completion at Authorization Boundaries}
\label{sec:boundaries}
All eleven primary configurations improve exactness and safe completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals (Figure~\ref{fig:completion}). For DeepSeek low/high, exact decisions rise from 248/400 (62.0\%) to 369/400 (92.25\%). The gain is 30.25 percentage points, with a 95\% template-cluster bootstrap interval of $[22.75,37.25]$, retaining both settings within each template. Exact purchases rise from 100 to 186 across 200 feasible outcomes; unsafe acceptances fall from 79 to 9 and false refusals from 53 to 1. On these tasks, stronger safety accompanies more useful purchases and fewer missed opportunities.

Gains cover all four contract families and 18 of 20 templates at each DeepSeek setting. They vary across models: GLM high improves from 64/200 to 188/200, Sol medium from 137/200 to 196/200, and Opus medium from 142/200 to 194/200. Qwen Max low rises from 59 to 87 exact outcomes but retains 46 unsafe acceptances. Appendix~\ref{app:statistics} gives complete partitions and template results. The paired design measures boundary responsiveness; its single feasible offer does not establish general ranking performance.

\label{sec:intent-decisions}

\subsection{Testing the Mechanisms behind Safe Choices}
\label{sec:permissions}
\label{sec:components}
\paragraph{Availability with authorized alternatives.}
We freeze 800 new requests from 20 templates in four contract families, each with 128 offers. DeepSeek Flash, GLM, and Qwen Max, all at low effort, receive requests, contracts, and schemas without catalogues. We retain the first fully evaluable specification per request and configuration. Catalogues are fixed before generation and stratified by whether their highest-reference-value offer is authorized (Appendix~\ref{app:availability}).

The primary stress test removes prefixes of the original generated ranking, without using reference labels to choose removals. Exposure requires an initially safe purchase to become unauthorized within eight removals, while an alternative remains permitted and positively valued under both generated conditions and references. Permissions, utilities, surviving attributes, and the selector stay fixed; replays make no model calls.

Among initially safe, fully evaluated purchases, \AvailHiddenPct\% conceal false admissions; the primary budget exposes \AvailExposedPct\% (Figure~\ref{fig:ablations}), spanning \AvailExposedRequests{} requests, \AvailExposedTemplates{} templates, all four families, and all three configurations. Minimal witnesses retain \AvailBackupMin{}--\AvailBackupMax{} viable authorized alternatives. Every exposed maximizer is unauthorized. Native-expression replay and independent contract checks verify the witnesses; Appendix~\ref{app:availability} reports random removals and reverse transitions.

\paragraph{An exact starting decision.}
\AvailExactExposed{} exposed specifications initially make exact decisions. In one GLM coupon-cancellation case, removing four offers causes an unauthorized purchase while 59 viable authorized alternatives remain. Its risk expression includes all 57 event terms but omits a group charge within one term. The true worst loss exceeds the limit by 100 subunits. The original exact choice had left this false permission untested (Appendix~\ref{app:availability}).

\paragraph{Correcting utility alone.}
With the catalogue and permission conditions fixed, Table~\ref{tab:oracle} examines 16 selected faulty Set outputs from one ambiguous ticket request. All select safe offers despite admitting an unauthorized candidate. Replacing only utility with the reference makes all 16 select an unauthorized offer: incorrect valuation had prevented its selection.

Each output omits Li's refund conditions and utility binding. Correcting permission alone repairs the domain but leaves the value error. Correcting both components, or restoring the omitted reading, recovers all 16 exact decisions; duplicating a label changes nothing. These reference-assisted interventions diagnose selected outputs, without estimating prevalence or implementing automatic repair (Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}).

\section{Conclusion}
SET separates authorization across retained states from worst-case value to make executable purchase-or-refusal decisions. On controlled tasks, it improves useful completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals. Frozen-specification tests expose hidden permissions through bounded removal of preferred offers, even while viable authorized alternatives remain. Separate component interventions show why correcting value cannot substitute for correcting permission. Faithful payment delegation requires checking the admitted domain alongside the selected purchase. Losing a preferred offer does not expand the user's authorization.
\label{mainend}

\section*{Limitations}
The evaluated contracts are synthetic, with complete catalogues, known attributes, correct item grounding, and finite state sets. Risk limits and utility are supplied as inputs. The main benchmark isolates authorization boundaries. Its unique feasible candidate provides limited evidence about ranking among several admissible purchases. The availability study instead retains multiple viable purchases and tests a constructed, reference-stratified distribution. Its directed-removal rates describe pressure on frozen specifications, not random stockouts or deployment frequencies. Intent tests signal unresolved readings explicitly. The evaluation does not cover open-ended interpretation discovery, hidden merchant terms, unknown inventory, fraud, or settlement.

Decision conditions can omit a relevant requirement or bind it to the wrong state, even when the resulting specification executes successfully. Reference implementations check consistency with stipulated semantics and share a development workflow. Configuration, language, and diagnostic panels overlap and do not provide independent task samples. Catalogue visibility and provider compute differ across some controls. The appendix specifies each cohort's execution and scoring protocol, including reference-assisted interventions. The reported gains concern the complete workflow. The selected-output interventions establish specific error mechanisms, not their frequency across requests or deployed systems.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
All purchases are synthetic, with no monetary transfers or customer records. Financial consequences follow stipulated benchmark contracts. Saved artifacts distinguish original responses, reference-assisted interventions, and subsequent analysis. In deployment, erroneous acceptance could cause financial loss, while erroneous refusal could prevent access to a needed service. A deployed system would need explicit authorization and failure handling. Correct execution alone cannot establish that a compiled specification faithfully represents the user's request.

\section{Decision Semantics and Implementation Details}

\subsection{Decision semantics and assumptions}
RAC supplies the fixed-set risk-averse decision principle \citep{rac}. The target decision problem fixes $x=(P,c)$, a nonempty retained state set $\Theta(x)$, and state-dependent hard requirements, risk permissions, and utilities. Utilities share a cardinal scale and are bounded over the finite experimental domain. An execution guarantee applies to this target only if the compiled specification preserves its decision-relevant semantics. End-to-end evaluation retains compilation errors. The formulation requires neither a unique reading nor assumptions about state probabilities, linearity, or convexity. We do not implement conformal coverage or action-conditional calibration.

\label{app:ambiguity}
The ticket trace in Table~\ref{tab:observed-trace} illustrates these semantics: refunding both travellers is compatible with either retained reading without resolving the referent or expanding the budget.

\subsection{Generated conditions and evaluation}
\label{app:interface}
The Set implementation uses a small expression language to encode the conditions used by Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision}. A specification $Q$ contains state-indexed clauses $(\widehat H_j,\widehat R_j,\widehat u_j)$. Each state record contains a label and expressions \texttt{hard}, \texttt{risk}, and \texttt{utility}. Boolean expressions support conjunction, disjunction, negation, comparisons, and conditionals. Arithmetic supports exact literals, basic operations, minimum, maximum, and absolute value. Boolean and numeric values have distinct types. Expression variables refer to attributes in the schema. A missing attribute needed for a feasibility check leaves that check uncertified. The primary benchmark provides complete attributes.

For the $K$ generated clauses, indexed by $j=1,\ldots,K$, evaluation gives
\begin{align}
\widehat f(z)&=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{K}(\widehat H_j(z)\wedge\widehat R_j(z)),\\
\widehat\psi(z)&=\min_{1\le j\le K}\widehat u_j(z).
\end{align}
The fixed procedure first requires complete enumeration of $C$, a decided $\widehat f(z)$ for every candidate, and known state utilities whenever $\widehat f(z)=1$. An aggregate predicate with a known false conjunct is false even if another conjunct is unknown. If these conditions cannot be established, the procedure returns \textsc{Fail}(uncertified) before selecting a purchase. The evaluator forms $\widehat F_C=\{z\in C:\widehat f(z)=1\}$ and computes the full maximizing set $Z^\star$. The response protocol and scorer permit any nonempty subset of tied positive-value maximizers with the correct guaranteed value. These are alternative complete purchases, of which one is selected. An empty domain or nonpositive maximum produces refusal. Invalid expressions produce failure rather than a certified refusal. Equivalent merged state expressions may encode the same decision functions.

\paragraph{Exactness on the declared domain.}
After these checks, membership in $\widehat F_C$ is decided for every $z\in C$, and all values over that domain are known. Finite enumeration therefore returns the complete set of maximizers when the maximum is positive. Otherwise, an empty domain or a nonpositive maximum proves that the compiled problem has no positive-value option. No unresolved candidate is silently excluded from the optimization.

Incomplete or uncertified execution counts as Other in the outcome protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}).

Concrete syntax, serialization, decoding, and runtime bounds are documented with the implementation in the artifact. The evaluation semantics and certification conditions above apply to the generated specification.

\subsection{Common instructions and outcome protocol}
\label{app:implementation}
All primary routes receive the same substantive policy, summarized below in English. The frozen model-facing prompts retain their original language and are unchanged by this summary.
\begin{quote}\small
Retain applicable interpretations. Respect current authorization in every retained state. Evaluate hard requirements and the complete risk permission, then maximize the minimum state-dependent utility. Buy only for positive guaranteed value; otherwise refuse. Do not ask the user or change permissions.
\end{quote}
Direct returns a decision, allowed identifiers, and guaranteed utility. Set returns the state-wise expressions of Appendix~\ref{app:interface}. Exactness requires agreement with the reference decision and, for acceptance, a nonempty subset of authorized maximizers and correct value. Disjoint failures are unsafe acceptance, false refusal, and Other (remaining value or invalid-execution failures). Set executions must satisfy the complete-domain checks in Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision}. Failed or unresolved execution counts as Other, including a no-action output whose reference decision is refusal.

\section{Controlled Decisions under Retained Intent Ambiguity}
\label{app:intent}
\subsection{Task semantics and controlled inputs}
The frozen parameter-boundary campaign contains three families with unresolved user interpretations. We include all three and exclude five families whose requirements are conditional but unambiguous. Selection is based on task semantics. This retrospective study is separate from the primary benchmark of contractual events.

\paragraph{Ticket-holder reference.}
Two male travellers need tickets, and ``his'' ticket must be refundable with a fee below the authorized limit. Utility penalizes that same traveller's delay and refund fee. Both referents remain possible. Each reading binds the hard requirement, risk bound, and value function to the corresponding traveller.

\paragraph{Hotel reference and conditional binding.}
Two guests need correctly registered rooms. ``His room'' must permit cancellation at 18:00 if he arrives at or after 18:00, or 24 hours before his expected arrival otherwise. The cancellation fee and utility weights refer to the same guest, so the unresolved antecedent affects conditional requirements and value.

\paragraph{Distributive versus collective scope.}
Gifts must satisfy recipients' preferences and wrapping requirements. ``Buy one for each; the exchange top-up is at most $r$'' may bound each recipient or the batch. Stipulated utility uses the corresponding maximum individual or total top-up. With nonnegative charges, the batch bound may dominate the individual bounds. The representation must still preserve the bound and score for each reading.

All requests explicitly signal unresolved reference or scope. Methods receive identical user text, complete catalogue attributes, synthetic utility formulas, and the common decision policy. Compilation in this historical panel is catalogue-visible; the primary Set protocol excludes the catalogue. Models receive no reference expressions, answers, or subsequent user clarification. The study evaluates decisions under the signaled ambiguity and does not test automatic discovery of unrestricted interpretations.

\subsection{Additional domain audits and exploratory checks}
\label{app:followup}
These checks examine whether executable conditions preserve authorization beyond the selected purchase. They are exploratory and use separate cohorts; they do not estimate deployment frequencies or extend the selected-output result in Table~\ref{tab:oracle} to a general trend.

\paragraph{Cohorts and complete-domain checks.}
The cross-structure cohort contains 24 requests: four families, three logical templates per family, and two parameter instances per template, with 64 offers per request. The families cover referent binding, conditional clauses, individual versus collective scope, and contractual events. DeepSeek Flash low, Qwen Max low (requested identifier \texttt{qwen3.8-max}), and GLM low each supply one specification per request without seeing the catalogue. All 72 specifications are auditable. Four local interventions independently use generated or reference permission and utility, with the request, catalogue order, and selector fixed. Reference utility is evaluated even for unauthorized offers. Utility correction causes no safe-to-unsafe switch on these catalogues. Two Qwen outputs nevertheless make exact decisions while admitting seven and three unauthorized offers; both concern conditional fee waivers.

We also audit all 1,080 Set outputs from the 18-setting effort panel. Of these, 1,024 support complete permission and utility evaluation. Three exact outputs have overbroad domains: Luna medium and Sol off on coupon cancellation, and Luna high on service replacement. Together with the two fee-waiver outputs, they cover three templates; all five remain safe after utility correction on their original catalogues. Greater reasoning effort generally improves both decisions and authorization domains in this panel. These observations separate current decision correctness from domain soundness without establishing that reasoning increases hidden errors.

\paragraph{Fixed-permission price changes.}
We lower one offer's price at a time by predefined 1\%, 2\%, 5\%, or 10\% steps, rounding the new price down to integer subunits and retaining every other offer. Reference consequences are recomputed. A valid intervention preserves reference authorization; the ranking-only subset also preserves generated authorization. In the 72-output cohort, 66 specifications permit independent price changes: 16,416 valid replays include 16,402 ranking-only replays, with no safe-to-unsafe switch under either generated or reference utility. The remaining six specifications have price tied to other contract attributes and are excluded.

In the effort archive, 586 specifications satisfy the stronger requirements for exact affine-price evaluation and unchanged permission guards, yielding 1,392,640 ranking-only replays. Exclusions comprise 56 non-auditable outputs and 438 with unsupported or linked price expressions. Generated utility produces one safe-to-unsafe and ten unsafe-to-safe transitions; reference utility produces one and fifteen, respectively. The two adverse transitions concern the same Luna high specification and price change, not two independent examples.

That service-replacement specification initially selects the reference-optimal offer with correct value. A 10\% price step reduces a different offer from 652,644 to 587,379 subunits. Its worst loss stays at 206,562, above the 200,000 limit, while the original authorized offer remains available. Both permission classifications remain unchanged. The discounted offer's reference utility rises from 1,140,794 to 1,206,059, exceeding the safe offer's 1,183,108. Thus it wins even with reference utility. Independent integer enumeration of all 57 events verifies the losses. This is one specification-level witness; 10\% is a tested grid point, not an exact minimum trigger.

\paragraph{Input and component checks.}
A separate 48-output pilot uses eight templates, Qwen Max low and GLM low, and three conditions: original input, an identical-input repeat, and a utility-base increase of 50,000 subunits. Catalogues, reference permissions, and optimal choices stay fixed. Compared with the original, Qwen changes one domain under both repetition and the value shift, removing the same seven false admissions; GLM changes one domain under repetition and none under the shift. Neither shift introduces a new false admission or a safe-to-unsafe switch. One syntactically invalid output was regenerated once, giving 49 attempts for 48 current auditable outputs; the first response is retained separately. This additive-shift test does not establish a broader preference effect.

\subsection{Historical frozen-rule replay}
\label{app:legacy-replay}
This earlier study tests the availability mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}: a frozen rule may admit an unauthorized offer without selecting it when a safe alternative is available.
We use a pooled collection of 648 saved decision specifications from our framework, covering 72 tasks under DeepSeek low, high, and max settings, with 216 outputs per setting. The tasks cross six templates, three authorization conditions, two catalogue sizes, and feasible/infeasible pairs. Each specification is evaluated on two new catalogues, giving 1,296 local replays without further model calls. Its decision conditions and selector remain fixed between replays, with no private reference information. Generation settings, catalogue visibility during preparation, and per-output provenance are indexed in the artifact. Legal candidate attributes are retained, administrative cost is set to zero, and only the first component's replacement/loss attribute changes. The mixed catalogue targets the following reference worst losses in integer subunits:
\begin{quote}\small
100000, 199999, 200000, 200001, 210000, 225000, 250000, 300000, 350000, 399999, 400000, 400001, 500000, 600000.
\end{quote}
The second catalogue contains only the 400,000-subunit offer, above the 200,000 limit. Other hard constraints remain satisfied. Numerical and symbolic references agree on 53,460 candidate--state checks. The catalogue changes are chosen to probe the generated consequence functions, so their outcomes do not estimate error rates in a randomly sampled purchasing population.



The results are grouped by effort setting. Outputs share tasks and are not treated as independent task samples.
