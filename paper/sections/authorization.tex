\section{Authorization-Error Diagnostics}
\label{app:permissionaudit}
\subsection{Prospective availability study}
\label{app:availability}
\paragraph{Tasks and retained specifications.}
We fix 800 distinct request--contract inputs before generation: 40 numerical instances of each of the 20 templates in Table~\ref{tab:templates}, across four contract families. Each has 128 candidates. DeepSeek Flash (\texttt{deepseek-flash}), GLM (\texttt{glm-5.3}), and Qwen Max (\texttt{qwen3.8-max}) use low effort and a 32,768-token output budget. Preparation receives the request, contract, and schema, excluding candidates and reference information.

For this diagnostic cohort, unchanged inputs are repeated as needed and the first fully evaluable specification is retained, without selecting on correctness, safety, or exposure. Full evaluability means that relevant permissions and required utilities are determined over the complete catalogue; it does not imply agreement with reference semantics. Table~\ref{tab:availability-models} specifies the resulting denominators. The artifact preserves the complete planned 2,400-unit list, every acquisition attempt, and the original single-attempt cohort. These diagnostic rates concern the retained specifications rather than single-attempt workflow reliability.

\paragraph{Catalogue construction and reference checks.}
The construction allocates 32 offers each to authorized positive-value purchases, unauthorized boundary cases, contract-branch controls, and ordinary sampled attributes. Construction categories differ from authorization labels: the latter two blocks may add authorized purchases. Boundary distances include one monetary subunit and approximately 1\%, 5\%, and 20\% of the risk limit. Attributes remain contract-consistent.

Each template has 20 S and 20 T requests. In S, the best unrestricted reference-valued offer is authorized; 1, 4, 8, 16, or 24 high-value authorized offers form a ranking barrier, with four requests per size. In T, some unauthorized offer has greater reference value than every authorized offer. These strata and all candidates are fixed before generation. The study therefore probes a controlled pressure distribution. Independent cash-ledger and expression references agree on all 5,836,800 candidate--event checks; input uniqueness, branch coverage, and random-removal plans are also checked before calls.

\paragraph{Frozen interventions and endpoint.}
The original catalogue, 128 random-removal conditions (32 each for $k\in\{1,4,8,16\}$), 16 generated-rank prefixes, and one unauthorized-only catalogue define 146 conditions per specification. Random plans are fixed in advance and shared across models. Ranked removals order originally admitted offers by generated utility, retaining original candidate order for ties; the removal rule uses no reference labels. Surviving attributes, permissions, utilities, and the selector remain unchanged. The unauthorized-only condition retains all reference-unauthorized offers and is an extreme control.

The primary endpoint requires an originally safe acceptance, an unauthorized selection for some prefix of at most eight removals, and at least one surviving offer permitted and positively valued under both generated conditions and the reference. Percentages use fully evaluable, originally safe purchases as the denominator. Each specification counts once per budget. Refusals do not count as safe purchases. Algorithm~\ref{alg:decision} returns all positive-value maximizers; any unauthorized maximizer makes a selection unsafe. Mixed-authorization ties and fixed-first selection are checked separately. Cumulative exposure is distinct from the outcome at exactly $k$ removals, which may return to safety after an unauthorized winner is itself removed.

\paragraph{Coverage, controls, and verification.}
The primary endpoint covers \AvailExposedRequests{} distinct requests, \AvailExposedTemplates{} templates, four families, and three configurations. All its witnesses occur in the predeclared S stratum; the family counts are 14 coupon, five insurance, ten return, and nineteen replacement cases. Coverage is uneven: DeepSeek's cases remain concentrated in service replacement. The endpoint holds with wholly unauthorized maximizing sets in every case, so no primary witness relies on a mixed-authorization tie. The minimum witnesses retain \AvailBackupMin{}--\AvailBackupMax{} viable authorized alternatives. Eight exposed outputs began with exact decisions, covering four templates and two model configurations.

Every observed transition and attainable minimum-deletion witness is independently selected from the candidate matrix and replayed through the original expressions. Native permissions and values must equal the corresponding subset of the original matrix. Certificates retain the violating reference loss, selected maximizing set, surviving alternatives, and source hashes. These checks validate the controlled mechanism; they do not estimate deployment frequency.

Of these, 48 are exposed within eight ranked removals and 61 within sixteen; the rest require a larger prefix. Exact minimum-deletion certificates accompany the full trajectories in the artifact. \paragraph{The four-removal, 59-alternative witness.}
The GLM a3 specification initially selects a reference maximizer with the correct value of 1,457,778 subunits. It contains one state record whose risk expression includes all 57 event terms. For event $\{0,1,3,5\}$, two partial-cancellation group charges must be added before the group cap: the correct capped charge is 19,500, but the expression includes only the second charge, yielding 9,900. Event coverage alone therefore does not ensure correct contractual aggregation.

After four originally higher-ranked offers become unavailable, the admitted unauthorized offer wins with generated value 1,359,878, ahead of the best surviving authorized value 1,257,378. Its true worst loss is 268,094 against a 267,994 limit; the specification's maximum is 263,194. The difference in this event's omitted charge is 9,600, whereas the difference between the two worst losses is 4,900; the maxima arise from different event calculations. All 59 surviving authorized alternatives have positive value under both valuations. The request, conditions, remaining attributes, and selector are unchanged. Original expressions, event ledgers, and the complete candidate-level certificate are provided in the artifact.

\subsection{Omitted-interpretation and component interventions}
\label{app:oracle}
The source pool contains 56 Qwen/GLM Set generations for one ambiguous ticket request and two catalogues (128 and 512 offers). We select sixteen outputs that admit a reference-unsafe candidate. Each omits the reading in which ``his'' refers to Li and retains only Zhang's fee-free-refund conditions and delay-weighted utility. These are 16 faulty outputs, not 16 independent requests. Restoring Li's state adds its conditions and utility. Duplicating Zhang's label leaves the missing semantics unchanged.

Table~\ref{tab:oracle} gives all six interventions and their outcomes, testing the distinction between permission and ranking in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}. Each replacement substitutes reference conditions or utilities while holding the remaining components fixed. State restoration supplies both for the omitted reading. All interventions are performed offline on the retained outputs.

\subsection{Two primary decision examples}
\label{app:witnesses}
Reference evaluation checks all 57 events. In one Astra Direct return-cost case, the reported loss of 1,954.33~MU understates the contractual loss of 2,054.33~MU against a 2,000~MU limit; no offer is feasible. In a coupon-cancellation case, Direct rejects all 1,024 offers although one has positive value and loss of 1,999.99~MU. These primary examples illustrate unsafe acceptance and false refusal at the same authorization boundary.

\subsection{Additional domain audits and exploratory checks}
\label{app:followup}
These checks examine whether executable conditions preserve authorization beyond the selected purchase. They are exploratory and use separate cohorts; they do not estimate deployment frequencies or extend the selected-output result in Table~\ref{tab:oracle} to a general trend.

\paragraph{Cohorts and complete-domain checks.}
The cross-structure cohort contains 24 requests: four families, three logical templates per family, and two parameter instances per template, with 64 offers per request. The families cover referent binding, conditional clauses, individual versus collective scope, and contractual events. DeepSeek Flash low, Qwen Max low (requested identifier \texttt{qwen3.8-max}), and GLM low each supply one specification per request without seeing the catalogue. All 72 specifications are auditable. Four local interventions independently use generated or reference permission and utility, with the request, catalogue order, and selector fixed. Reference utility is evaluated even for unauthorized offers. Utility correction causes no safe-to-unsafe switch on these catalogues. Two Qwen outputs nevertheless make exact decisions while admitting seven and three unauthorized offers; both concern conditional fee waivers.

We also audit all 1,080 Set outputs from the 18-setting effort panel. Of these, 1,024 support complete permission and utility evaluation. Three exact outputs have overbroad domains: Luna medium and Sol off on coupon cancellation, and Luna high on service replacement. Together with the two fee-waiver outputs, they cover three templates; all five remain safe after utility correction on their original catalogues. Greater reasoning effort generally improves both decisions and authorization domains in this panel. These observations separate current decision correctness from domain soundness without establishing that reasoning increases hidden errors.

\paragraph{Fixed-permission price changes.}
We lower one offer's price at a time by predefined 1\%, 2\%, 5\%, or 10\% steps, rounding the new price down to integer subunits and retaining every other offer. Reference consequences are recomputed. A valid intervention preserves reference authorization; the ranking-only subset also preserves generated authorization. In the 72-output cohort, 66 specifications permit independent price changes: 16,416 valid replays include 16,402 ranking-only replays, with no safe-to-unsafe switch under either generated or reference utility. The remaining six specifications have price tied to other contract attributes and are excluded.

In the effort archive, 586 specifications satisfy the stronger requirements for exact affine-price evaluation and unchanged permission guards, yielding 1,392,640 ranking-only replays. Exclusions comprise 56 non-auditable outputs and 438 with unsupported or linked price expressions. Generated utility produces one safe-to-unsafe and ten unsafe-to-safe transitions; reference utility produces one and fifteen, respectively. The two adverse transitions concern the same Luna high specification and price change, not two independent examples.

That service-replacement specification initially selects the reference-optimal offer with correct value. A 10\% price step reduces a different offer from 652,644 to 587,379 subunits. Its worst loss stays at 206,562, above the 200,000 limit, while the original authorized offer remains available. Both permission classifications remain unchanged. The discounted offer's reference utility rises from 1,140,794 to 1,206,059, exceeding the safe offer's 1,183,108. Thus it wins even with reference utility. Independent integer enumeration of all 57 events verifies the losses. This is one specification-level witness; 10\% is a tested grid point, not an exact minimum trigger.

\paragraph{Input and component checks.}
A separate 48-output pilot uses eight templates, Qwen Max low and GLM low, and three conditions: original input, an identical-input repeat, and a utility-base increase of 50,000 subunits. Catalogues, reference permissions, and optimal choices stay fixed. Compared with the original, Qwen changes one domain under both repetition and the value shift, removing the same seven false admissions; GLM changes one domain under repetition and none under the shift. Neither shift introduces a new false admission or a safe-to-unsafe switch. One syntactically invalid output was regenerated once, giving 49 attempts for 48 current auditable outputs; the first response is retained separately. This additive-shift test does not establish a broader preference effect.

\subsection{Historical frozen-rule replay}
\label{app:legacy-replay}
This earlier study tests the availability mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}: a frozen rule may admit an unauthorized offer without selecting it when a safe alternative is available.
We use a pooled collection of 648 saved decision specifications from our framework, covering 72 tasks under DeepSeek low, high, and max settings, with 216 outputs per setting. The tasks cross six templates, three authorization conditions, two catalogue sizes, and feasible/infeasible pairs. Each specification is evaluated on two new catalogues, giving 1,296 local replays without further model calls. Its decision conditions and selector remain fixed between replays, with no private reference information. Generation settings, catalogue visibility during preparation, and per-output provenance are indexed in the artifact. Legal candidate attributes are retained, administrative cost is set to zero, and only the first component's replacement/loss attribute changes. The mixed catalogue targets the following reference worst losses in integer subunits:
\begin{quote}\small
100000, 199999, 200000, 200001, 210000, 225000, 250000, 300000, 350000, 399999, 400000, 400001, 500000, 600000.
\end{quote}
The second catalogue contains only the 400,000-subunit offer, above the 200,000 limit. Other hard constraints remain satisfied. Numerical and symbolic references agree on 53,460 candidate--state checks. The catalogue changes are chosen to probe the generated consequence functions, so their outcomes do not estimate error rates in a randomly sampled purchasing population.



The results are grouped by effort setting. Outputs share tasks and are not treated as independent task samples.
