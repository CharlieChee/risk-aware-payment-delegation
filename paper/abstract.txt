Delegated purchasing must respect existing authorization when interpretations or contractual outcomes remain uncertain. We formulate purchase or refusal without further user interaction by separating joint authorization from worst-case utility. SET selects a purchase admissible across retained states with the highest positive worst-case value, or refuses. On synthetic contractual-boundary tasks across model families, it improves exact decisions and useful completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals relative to direct decisions under the same policy. Yet an unauthorized alternative may be admitted but lose in ranking. A prospective study freezes decision conditions and varies candidate availability: removing a few high-ranked offers exposes unauthorized purchases across contract families, even while alternatives remain authorized and positively valued under both the specification and the reference. One specification starts from an exact decision but purchases an unauthorized offer after four removals, with 59 viable authorized alternatives remaining. Separate reference-assisted interventions on selected faulty outputs show how correcting utility can also expose overbroad permissions. These analyses distinguish correctness of the selected purchase from soundness of the admitted domain: losing a preferred offer does not expand the user's authorization.
