Delegated purchasing must respect existing authorization when interpretations or contractual outcomes remain uncertain. We formulate purchase or refusal without further user interaction by separating joint authorization from worst-case utility. SET selects a purchase admissible across retained states with the highest positive worst-case value, or refuses when none qualifies. On synthetic contractual-boundary tasks across model families, it improves exact decisions and useful completion while reducing unsafe acceptances and false refusals relative to direct decisions under the same policy. Yet a correct current choice does not certify the permissions encoded by the generated conditions. We analyze how ranking conceals false admissions and test this distinction by freezing specifications and varying candidate availability. Across 800 requests and three model configurations, removing at most eight high-ranked offers exposes unauthorized purchases on 48 requests spanning 15 contract templates and all four families. Every witness retains alternatives authorized and positively valued under both generated conditions and references; eight begin with exact decisions. These findings connect intent fidelity to executable decisions and motivate checking the admitted domain alongside the selected purchase: losing a preferred offer does not expand the user's authorization.
